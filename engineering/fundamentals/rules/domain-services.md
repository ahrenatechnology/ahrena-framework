# Behaviour that belongs to no aggregate

Four conditions, all decided by a reviewer. No script decides any of them, three were written and measured before that was accepted, and the final section gives the numbers. `docs/domain-services.md` explains what each condition protects, carries those measurements in full, and records why the two patterns named beside this one in the same piece of work got a catalog entry instead of a rule.

**The subject is the residue a correctly drawn boundary leaves.** `rules/aggregates.md` draws the consistency boundary and says what crosses it. Drawing it by the invariants, as its condition 1 requires, produces behaviour that fits inside no boundary: a rule of the business whose terms name two roots at once. Nothing in this plugin said where that behaviour goes, and both defaults are wrong. Pushed into one of the two roots, it makes that root know about the other, which is condition 3 of `rules/aggregates.md` arriving by the back door. Pushed up into the application layer, it stops being part of the model — it is now a step in a procedure, invisible to anyone reading the domain to learn the rules, and it will be written a second time by whoever builds the next procedure.

**The layout is inherited rather than declared here.** A bounded context is a directory and `domain/` inside it is policy; condition 1 of `rules/domain-model.md` states that and `hooks/check-structure.py` already enforces the import half of it. This rule adds the other direction — what must be *in* the domain — and restates none of the first.

No condition here adds an abstraction, so the trigger in condition 1 of `rules/yagni.md` gates none of them. Condition 1 is the one that looks gated, because naming a service usually introduces a type. It is not, for the reason `docs/simplicity.md` gives about the conditions that remove structure: the state the condition displaces is a root reaching into another root, which is larger than the state that replaces it and is already forbidden. Where extracting a service does introduce a port, because the service must reach a second aggregate it cannot import, that port is gated — and the gating is condition 6 of `rules/solid.md` and the port entry of `docs/patterns.md`, which is where it already lives.

## Conditions

1. **Behaviour whose terms name two aggregate roots belongs to neither of them.** The state is an operation on one root that takes another root, that root's repository, or a collection of them, as a parameter, and decides something the business would state as one rule. Evans defines the service as an operation that conceptually belongs to no object; the two-root case is the one a boundary drawn by invariants produces, and it is the one that arises here. The number is two and it is not a threshold — at one root the behaviour is a method and this condition has no subject. Condition 3 of `rules/aggregates.md` forbids the *field*; this forbids the *parameter*, which is the same coupling with nothing in the type declaration to show for it. Decided by a reviewer, or by a script once the project names the base type its aggregates inherit — the same input every condition in `rules/aggregates.md` asks for.

2. **A domain service holds no state between operations.** The state is a field written anywhere but construction, or a field that carries the outcome of one call into the next. A service is defined by what it does rather than by what it is, so a service that remembers is an aggregate nobody named, and it is the shape that fails under concurrency, because every caller shares the one instance. The collaborators handed to it at construction are not state in this sense; they are wiring, and they are fixed for the object's life. Evans states the stateless requirement directly, and it is taken unchanged. Decided by a reviewer.

3. **A domain service sits under the context's `domain/` directory and its signature names only domain types.** The state is behaviour that decides a rule of the business, living under `application/`, `adapters/`, `infrastructure/` or `persistence/`. The test is whether the operation would still have to exist if every delivery mechanism were replaced: if it would, it is a model of the business and belongs where the model is. Condition 1 of `rules/domain-model.md` decides the converse — a domain module importing a mechanism — with a script, and nothing here repeats it. Decided by a reviewer.

4. **An operation that only loads, calls and saves is not a domain service.** The state is a type under `domain/` whose every operation fetches through a port, invokes one aggregate, persists the result, and decides nothing of its own. That is a use case: it sequences, and sequencing is the application layer's job. The refusal is inherited rather than invented — the predecessor framework's own DDD reference lists "avoid when it is only IO orchestration" against Domain Service, and it is the line in its tactical table worth carrying over. A service under `domain/` that orchestrates has also usually taken an import that condition 1 of `rules/domain-model.md` fails, which is the cheap half of the same finding. Decided by a reviewer.

## Where this stops

**Condition 1 does not reach a query.** An operation that loads two roots to answer a question and changes neither is a read model, and `docs/patterns.md` already says a projection holds whatever its consumer needs. The condition is about behaviour that decides, and the test is whether anything would be written differently as a result.

**Condition 1 does not make every two-argument function a service.** A function that formats two roots for a screen, serialises them or compares them for a test touches two and decides nothing the business would recognise. What the condition looks for is a rule somebody could state in a sentence without mentioning the code, and whose sentence names both.

**Condition 2 does not reach a memo or a lazily built table.** A service that caches an expensive derivation holds state that no caller can observe as behaviour, and forbidding it buys nothing. The state the condition is about is state that changes what the next call answers.

**Condition 3 does not reach a tree with no `domain/` directory.** The layer is found by the directory name, so a project laid out differently gets nothing from this condition and supplies its own map to a reviewer. That is the same cost `rules/domain-model.md` pays for choosing a convention over a configuration, and the same answer: a configurable layer map drifts from the layout and is wrong invisibly.

**Condition 4's line is thin, and the rule says so rather than pretending.** Between "only sequences" and "decides something" is where most arguments about services happen, and a use case almost always contains one small decision — which of two adapters, whether to skip on an empty result. The condition fires on the type whose every operation is a sequence, not on the one that sequences and also decides. Where a reviewer genuinely cannot tell, `rules/yagni.md`'s position applies: the smaller code wins, and the smaller code here is one fewer type in the domain.

**A naming condition was considered, measured and refused.** The obvious fifth condition is a suffix test: a `*Service` type must satisfy conditions 2 to 4. Run over the CPython 3.11 standard library — 564 files, 288,485 lines, the corpus `docs/duplication.md` measured its own floors against — a detector for `*Service`, `*Policy`, `*Calculator`, `*Manager` or `*Handler` holding state outside construction reports **29 classes, and all 29 are correct code**: `logging`'s handler hierarchy, `http.cookiejar.DefaultCookiePolicy`, `socketserver`'s request handlers. Narrowed to `*Service` and `*Policy` it reports 2, and both are still correct. The suffix names a framework's vocabulary rather than this codebase's, which is the case condition 4 of `rules/pattern-selection.md` already carves out, and a list of type-name suffixes is the detector `docs/clean-code.md` refuses for naming on the grounds that it is wrong in both directions. One measurement and two standing positions agree, so the condition is not here.

**This rule declares judgment rather than a hook, and three detectors were written before that was accepted.** A stateless class with exactly one method — the shape a domain service is most often wrongly given — reports 14 classes over the same 288,485 lines, and not one is a service: they are sentinels and dummies, a metaclass, a mixin, the terminating leaf of `pathlib`'s selector chain, two test scaffolds both named `_C`, an SAX interface base and two documentation examples. The suffix detector is above. The third, for condition 1, is runnable and needs the aggregate-root marker this plugin cannot guess, which is exactly where `rules/aggregates.md` ended and for exactly the same reason. `docs/domain-services.md` carries the runs. What is left is four conditions a reader decides, so the rule declares judgment and its statement is injected, rather than a script standing in for it and being confidently wrong.

**The two patterns named beside this one got no rule, and that is a result rather than an omission.** Specification and factory were examined in the same piece of work. Specification's failure mode is one selection rule written in a repository query and again in an in-memory filter, which is the "same decision" definition of duplication that `rules/duplication.md` refused from its own conditions after measuring it; the narrowest detector that survives reports one true finding in twenty-five. Factory's failure mode is an aggregate assembled field by field at a call site, which condition 1 of `rules/aggregates.md` already forbids as an invariant enforced outside its boundary; the marker-free detector reports nine sites in 288,485 lines and is wrong in all nine, because it cannot tell a call site from the factory function whose job is exactly that assembly. Both are named fixes with a situation, a cost and a cheaper default, so both are entries in `docs/patterns.md`, which is what that catalog is for. `docs/domain-services.md` carries both refusals with their numbers.

**Nothing here parses anything, so the language caveat the other rules carry does not apply.** `rules/kiss.md`, `rules/duplication.md` and `rules/domain-model.md` each end by saying their detector reads Python and nothing else. This rule has no detector to be Python-shaped, which means it costs the same in every language and delivers the same in every language: a reader.

**The footprint is the cost, and it is 152 characters.** A judgment rule's `statement` is injected into every request, including those with no domain model in them. This plugin carried 1,001 characters across seven judgment rules before this one, as `rules/aggregates.md` accounts for and `docs/context-budget.md` in the foundation plugin measures. Eight rules and 1,153 characters is the figure to weigh against what the rule delivers, and the honest summary is the one `rules/aggregates.md` gave: the alternative was not a cheaper enforcement route but no rule at all. Two of the three patterns this rule arrived with took that alternative.
