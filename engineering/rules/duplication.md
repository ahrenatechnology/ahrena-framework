# Duplication at the third copy

Two conditions, both decided by `hooks/check-structure.py`. Each is a shape in the source rather than an opinion about it, which is what lets this rule cost nothing to load and still fire. `docs/duplication.md` gives the three things "a duplicate" can mean, the argument for the one that shipped, the measurements that placed both floors, and the third condition that measurement removed.

**The arbitration is inherited, not invented.** Three occurrences is the rule of three that condition 4 of `rules/solid.md` uses for the discriminator chain, condition 2 of `rules/value-semantics.md` for the data clump and condition 5 of `rules/cross-cutting-concerns.md` for the boundary. `docs/simplicity.md` records the position that an arbitration mechanism coming in two incompatible versions is not one, and that applies with more force to a fourth. At the first occurrence there is code, at the second a coincidence, at the third a decision nobody has written down. The script carries the number once, as `RULE_OF_THREE`, and every condition that needs it reads it from there.

**A duplicate here is a repeated shape.** Not a repeated token, which is the same thing minus every copy somebody renamed a variable in, and not a repeated decision, which is the definition worth having and which no script decides. `docs/duplication.md` argues both refusals; the second is the more important one and this rule's boundary section states what it costs.

Condition 1's fix can add a parameter or a seam, so it is gated by the abstraction trigger declared in condition 1 of `rules/yagni.md`. A rule may reference only a doc, so the trigger is named here in prose rather than linked. It reads: an abstraction is justified when it has a second real consumer, or when a test that exists today requires the seam. Being the third occurrence, the gate is satisfied by construction and the finding says so in one sentence. Condition 2 replaces three copies of a table with one declaration and an import, and a name is not an abstraction, so the trigger does not reach it.

## Conditions

Each of these is decided by `hooks/check-structure.py`.

1. **The third function body with the same shape is a duplicate.** Two bodies have the same shape when either can be obtained from the other by renaming. Identifiers and literals are renamed consistently rather than erased — the first distinct name becomes `n0` and the second `n1` — so `a + b` stays a different shape from `a + a` and only a real copy collides. A body of fewer than **5 statements** is not counted, measured the way `docs/clean-code.md` measured its own threshold: over 288,485 lines of the CPython standard library, four statements is where the detector first groups code that is alike by accident, and every group at five and above is a genuine repetition. The count runs over every file the hook is given, in one pass, and inside one bounded context — a context is a directory, as condition 1 of `rules/domain-model.md` declares, and three copies split across two of them are two decisions that agree today. Gated, and satisfied by construction: the third occurrence is the second consumer and then some.

2. **The third module-level table with the same contents is a duplicate.** The state is a module-level assignment binding a list, set, tuple or dict display of **3 or more entries** — or a single-argument call around one, so that `frozenset({...})` and `tuple([...])` count as the tables they are — whose contents appear in 3 or more places inside one context. The contents are compared and the name is not, because the name is what a copy changes and the contents are what has to stay in agreement. Three entries is Fowler's Data Clumps figure, the same one condition 2 of `rules/value-semantics.md` takes for the parameter run; below it the detector reports `{}`, `[]` and `['Popen']`, and `docs/duplication.md` carries the counts. Not gated.

## Where this stops

**Neither condition reaches the duplicate that shares no shape.** A rounding rule written once in the domain and once in the report, a validation written as a regular expression in one place and a chain of comparisons in another, an enum mirrored by a check constraint in a migration: these are the same decision expressed twice, they are the expensive form of duplication, and nothing here detects them. That is a refusal rather than a gap to close later. Deciding it means deciding that two different programs compute the same thing, and the version a script can actually run is a similarity score with a threshold on it — a detector that is wrong often enough to teach people to switch the gate off, which `rules/yagni.md` argues against and which already cost `rules/kiss.md` a condition. `docs/duplication.md` carries the question a reviewer asks instead of the detector this rule will not ship.

**Condition 1 does not reach a copy that has drifted.** A body with one statement inserted or deleted has a different shape and is not reported, and that is the state of most copies a year after they were made. The detector finds a copy while it is still identical, which is while it is cheap to collapse, and loses it once the copies have started to diverge, which is when collapsing them is a negotiation. Closing that gap needs an edit distance over shapes and a threshold on the distance, and a tunable threshold is the thing this rule was able to avoid by taking the rule of three instead.

**Condition 1 does not reach a body under the floor, however many copies there are.** A four-statement body repeated forty times is invisible. At that size the shape is reached by accident: in the corpus the floor was measured against, four statements is where three unrelated constructors in `argparse`, `difflib` and `doctest` collide because each assigns four parameters to four fields. The floor buys silence on coincidence at the price of blindness to small duplicates, and it is the same trade `rules/clean-code.md` makes with its statement count.

**Condition 1 reads whole bodies, not the block inside one.** The same eight lines pasted into the middle of three longer functions are duplication and are invisible here. The unit is the body because the fix for a duplicated body is to call the one that exists, while the fix for a duplicated window is to extract a function that does not — which is the move the abstraction trigger gates, and which is wrong often enough that a detector proposing it on every matching window would fail more reviews than it passed. This is argued rather than measured, and `docs/duplication.md` says so.

**A finding is a question, and two of them in the measured corpus have "no" for an answer.** `throw` on a generator and `athrow` on an async generator have one shape and cannot have one body. `pathlib`'s seven `is_dir`, `is_file`, `is_symlink` and siblings differ only in the predicate each passes, and collapsing them costs seven readable names to save six statements. Both are real repetitions; in both the collapse is worse than the duplication. Exclude by path and write down why, as `rules/kiss.md` and `rules/cross-cutting-concerns.md` already ask for their own carve-outs.

**The two copies in this framework's own tree are the case the rule of three protects.** `SKIP_DIRS` is declared identically in `engineering/hooks/check-structure.py` and `engineering-python/hooks/check-boundaries.py`, and the `case` helper is written out in four test suites. Nothing fires, because two is not three and because the helper is one statement. A third plugin would make the table a finding, and the finding would be correct and the fix would still be refused: a shared module between plugins is the dependency the no-dependency constraint exists to prevent. The rule of three delays that argument; it does not settle it.

**Test trees are counted like everything else, and often should not be.** Three tests whose bodies differ only in a literal are a table and should be one; three tests that repeat a shape so each reads on its own are doing the thing tests are for, and collapsing them hides the case behind a helper. The condition cannot tell those apart and does not try. Exclude the test tree by path, the way condition 2 of `rules/cross-cutting-concerns.md` asks for the fixture that commits and rolls back.

**Condition 2 does not reach a table that is not written down.** A vocabulary built by a comprehension, read from a file at import time or assembled from another module's constants has no literal to compare, and two modules deriving the same set from the same source are not reported. What the condition catches is the copy that was typed, which is the copy that goes out of agreement.

**This rule declares a hook rather than judgment, and it follows `rules/value-semantics.md` rather than `rules/solid.md`.** Both conditions are decided by a parser, so the script stands in for the rule and the rule contributes no text to any request. That is the cheapest route and it matters more here than usual: the always-loaded footprint is 847 characters across six judgment rules, up from 281 across two, and `docs/context-budget.md` in the foundation plugin is the accounting. The gate on condition 1 would normally be the argument for `judgment`, as it is in `rules/solid.md` — a hook rule contributes no text, so the arbitration would be dropped. It is not dropped, for the reason `rules/value-semantics.md` gives about its own two gated conditions: the gate has a single answer, the answer fits in a sentence, and the finding carries it at the moment the condition fires rather than in every request that has nothing to do with duplication. The difference from `rules/solid.md` is where the undecidable material went. That rule numbers four conditions a script cannot decide and must declare judgment to deliver them; the definition of a duplicate that no script can decide was refused from this rule rather than numbered inside it, and lives in `docs/duplication.md`, which is the move `rules/clean-code.md` makes with naming. It is also not the `rules/contract-first.md` case, where every detector is runnable and each needs an input this plugin cannot guess: both floors here are constants, and the layout condition 1 needs to draw a context boundary is a directory name that `rules/domain-model.md` already declares.

**The detector parses Python and nothing else.** The standard library ships one parser and this plugin takes no dependencies. In any other language these two conditions are read by a reviewer, which is the weaker route and is stated here rather than left to be discovered.
